The source rail

SOP 02 §18 requires every material item to be classified and the classification to survive into the client packet. A narrow rail runs down the left of every fact block carrying one mark. Choosing the mark is how a fact gets written — there is no unmarked path.

 Verified MLS fact
Retrieved from Northstar/Matrix this session. Solid navy.
 Public record in Matrix
County or assessor data displayed inside Matrix. Half navy.
 Listing-reported
The listing or its agent says so. Not independently confirmed. Hollow.
 Calculated
Derived here from stated figures — price per sq ft, NOI, cap rate.
 Requires verification
Not yet established. Dashed red. Never phrased as a defect.
 Blaise's observation
Professional judgment, labeled as judgment.
 Your priority
What the buyer told Blaise. Never inferred, never invented.
Never printed
Agent-only remarks. They stay in the research packet and never reach a client page.

Typography

Fraunces for the address and page titles — a serif with enough character to read as personally produced. Public Sans for everything a buyer reads at length. IBM Plex Mono for every number, label and source mark, so figures align in columns and labels never compete with prose.

What stands out

Four things worth your attention before you walk in.

  • Two bedrooms, one bathroom, at $125,000

    The configuration and price shown in ShowingTime put this at the entry end of the Milaca market. That is the fact. Whether it is priced correctly depends on square footage, condition and year built — none of which are confirmed yet.

  • A single-bathroom home narrows your resale pool

    Not a reason to walk away at this price point. It is a reason to look hard at whether a second bathroom could ever be added, and where it would go.

  • The condition question is the whole question here

    At this price, the difference between a home that needs cosmetics and one that needs mechanicals is the difference between a good buy and a money pit. Everything on the verify list below points at that one question.

  • Market time is not yet established

    Days on market, price history and any prior listing activity have not been retrieved from Matrix. If this home has sat, that changes the negotiating picture materially — and it is the first thing I will pull afterward.

Your plan, as you have described it

Buy the lot now and build later, using cash, land financing, or a combination depending on how much cash you want to deploy. You already know this property. So this packet skips the introduction and goes straight to what could complicate the build.

What stands out

  • Utilities are listed available — none listed connected

    Electric, natural gas, sewer, water and phone all show as available. Not one shows as connected. The gap between those two words is a real number in your build budget, and nobody has quoted it yet.

  • Level, paved-street, platted lot with no MLS restrictions

    Topography level, street paved, restrictions field empty, light tree coverage. For a build site those are the easy answers, and this lot has them.

  • Corners are marked; a prior survey exists but has not been found

    Listing-side statement. Marked corners are not a boundary survey, and a survey nobody can produce is not a survey you own.

  • The house next door may come to market under $300,000

    Reported by the listing agent, who says he owns it. Not verified as an active listing — no address, price, timing or condition confirmed. Worth asking about, not worth planning around yet.

Why this one matters

The fee covers electric and gas, which most units in this association do not. $876 is high in absolute terms, but part of it is utility cost you would otherwise pay separately. Compare fee plus expected utilities between units, never the fee alone.
